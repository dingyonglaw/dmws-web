    Design Notes

    Who it's for

    Children aged 9 to 12. The goal is to learn steady pulse, tempo, and rhythmic patterns through play, with only two thumbs. Comedy and villains bring them back. The rhythm practice is the real game underneath.

    Keep the heartbeat going

    • One long run per chapter. Boot Camp is four drills in one continuous march, and Chapter 1 is four zones in one run. Story is spoken during the march (Sgt. Salt on the radio, nuggets in speech bubbles), and comics only play at a chapter's start and end.
    • Failing never stops the music. When the whole squad flops, the game shows "SHAKE IT OFF!", the squad pops back at the last checkpoint, and a 1-2-3-4 count brings the player back in on the same tempo.
    • Graduation keeps the beat. At the end of Boot Camp the music keeps playing while the player chooses: keep practising on the same field, or start Chapter 1.
    • A personal tempo. Tempo is a global setting, 120 BPM by default (the classic drum-march-game tempo). During Boot Camp and practice, − and + buttons beside the commands change it on the fly without breaking the beat.

    Learning principles

    • One new idea per drill: MARCH, then ATTACK, DODGE and DIP. Commands unlock as you walk into each drill.
    • See the beat, then feel it. The beat track shows counts 1 to 4 (your turn) and 5 to 8 (the army sings it back). Each tap leaves a mark where it landed, so early and late are visible.
    • Ghost notes suggest the next command in training and when the Helper Drum is on. They can be set to Always or Off.
    • The Helper Drum (10 BPM slower, wider timing, softer hits) turns on after two flops. It is offered without shame and can be switched in Pause.
    • Three stars: finish, 75% of taps on the beat, and reach Fever.
    • Mistakes are funny. Fallen nuggets give dramatic last words, then pop back up with "JUST KIDDING!"

    Commands

    CommandPatternEffect
    MARCHBONK BONK BONK HONKWalk forward
    ATTACKHONK HONK BONK HONKCharge at a nearby enemy, then hit twice (three times in Fever)
    DODGEBONK HONK BONK HONKDuck and cover: 80% less damage for 8 beats. If a Brute is in your face, the squad also scrambles back a few steps.
    DIPHONK BONK BONK BONKHeal, and wake flopped nuggets

    Rescued squad types: Corn Dog Lancer (Ch.1, rewards the charge), Flag Bearer (Ch.2, rewards perfect timing), Saucerer (Ch.3, rewards long chains with Sauce Storm and Sauce Bubble), Popcorn Archer (Ch.4, off-beats).

    Fever: every chained command adds 1 to the meter, or 2 if all four taps were perfect. FEVER at 3, SUPER at 6, MAX at 10. A mistake only drops you one level. Fever is also armor: the squad takes 20% less damage in FEVER, 35% in SUPER and half in MAX, so staying on the beat keeps you alive.

    Bosses: boss attacks deal half damage, and bosses have much more health (Baron ×4, Grand Tofu ×6, Queen and Sir Florette ×8), so a boss fight is a long rhythm battle instead of two hits. If the squad flops, the boss keeps the damage you already did.

    Story

    • The Misfit Nuggets: Crunchy (brave bandana), Sir Dips-a-Lot (ketchup-cup helmet), Kevin (just Kevin), Nuggie (party hat) and Breadcrumb Bob (chef hat, notices clues). The Perfect Nugget Factory rejected all five.
    • Sgt. Salt is a grumpy drill-sergeant shaker who secretly believes in them.
    • Chapter 1: the Big Drum and the Sacred Gravy Boat are stolen. The squad marches through Crouton Fields, Pea Pod Valley and Toaster Bridge to the Baron's lettuce castle. Cliffhanger: the Baron was only working for Queen Kaleopatra, who now has the Big Drum.
    • Finale: Emperor Quinoa once quit drumming after one mistake that everyone laughed at. The nuggets don't defeat him. They invite him to jam.

    Tech

    • All audio is made live with Web Audio, and the audio clock is the source of truth for timing. A tempo change moves the clock at the next beat that hasn't been scheduled yet, so the beat never skips.
    • Everything is in one HTML file: Canvas 2D drawing, multi-touch, and progress and settings saved in the browser.
